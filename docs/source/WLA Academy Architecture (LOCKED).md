## WLA ACADEMY ARCHITECTURE FINAL

## Core Platform Architecture

## Purpose

The WLA Academy Architecture defines the shared platform structure and behaviour that applies across missions.

It owns the common learner journey, access model, mission repository structure, family-facing surfaces, progress behaviour and Academy-wide destinations.

Individual mission Build Briefs inherit this architecture and specify only what is unique to that

mission.

## Governing principle

Complexity belongs in the system. Clarity belongs in the experience.

The Academy should make missions easy to access, prepare for, enter, continue and complete

without taking over the meaningful work of the mission.

## 1. Public website / Academy boundary

The public website exists to help families:

discover → understand → choose → purchase

The Academy exists to help children and families:

access → prepare → engage → continue → complete

## Public primary navigation

Home | Missions | Labs | Journal | About

## Separate functional routes

My Missions | Try a Free Mission | Reviews | Buy a Gift | Redeem a Gift

These routes sit outside the primary marketing navigation because they serve direct functional

purposes.

## Academy-side destination

Mission Board

Mission Board belongs within the Academy ecosystem, not the public functional-route layer.

## 2. Core Academy journey

The shared mission journey is:

Purchase / Unlock → My Missions → Mission Home → Active Mission → Mission Complete →

Mission Trail → My Missions

This is the primary flow and should remain visually and functionally clear.

Mission Board sits beneath Mission Home as an optional Academy-wide destination.

It is not a required step in the mission journey.

## 3. Account and child ownership

The Academy uses a family account model.

## Parent / guardian

The parent or guardian:

owns the account, access and permissions

This includes account-level matters such as:

· purchases and mission entitlements; · account management; · child-profile management; · parent guidance;

· privacy and consent permissions.

## Child profile

Each child profile:

owns its mission progress and learning record

This includes:

· Not Started / In Progress / Complete status; · mission-specific state; · resume position; · saved digital responses; · Mission Trail evidence associated with that child.

One parent account may support more than one child profile.

Mission progress should therefore not be stored only against the parent account.

Where more than one child profile exists, the active child profile must be clear before mission access, and My Missions must show the missions and progress belonging to that child.

Mission access must be associated with the intended child profile before child-specific progress

begins.

## 4. My Missions

My Missions is the child/family collection of available missions.

It answers:

What missions do I have? What was I doing? Where do I continue?

## Each mission card should show

· mission title; · Lab; · age range; · approximate duration; · status; · primary action.

## Core statuses

Not started

In progress

Complete

## Primary actions

Not started:

Open Mission

In progress:

Continue

Complete:

View Mission

## Behaviour

- purchased, unlocked or redeemed missions appear here; · status persists; · completed missions remain accessible; · returning users can resume without searching for previous links or emails.

My Missions should remain focused on mission access and status.

It should not become a repository for every mission file, parent guidance or Mission Trail item.

## 5. Mission Home

Every mission has one permanent Mission Home.

Mission Home is the bridge between:

owning a mission

and

doing the mission

It remains available before, during and after mission completion.

Mission Home is

primarily child-accessible, but family-readable

The child should be able to independently open, prepare for and continue a mission where practical.

A parent should also be able to find preparation information and guidance without needing a

separate parent dashboard.

## Required Mission Home areas

- 1. Start / Continue Mission

- 2. Mission Kit

- 3. For Parents

## Visual hierarchy

Start / Continue Mission is the strongest action.

Mission Kit and For Parents remain clearly accessible but subordinate.

## 6. Start / Continue Mission

Before the mission begins:

Start Mission

Once progress exists:

Continue Mission

After completion:

show the mission as Complete rather than automatically restarting it.

Start / Continue opens the Active Mission environment.

## 7. Mission Kit

Mission Kit is the permanent home for resources supplied by WLA for that mission.

Possible contents include:

- Child Mission; · Town Map; · cards;

- trackers;

- mission-specific printables;

- other required physical resources.

Exact contents vary by mission.

## Resource actions

As appropriate:

View

Print

Download

## Behaviour

Mission Kit resources remain available:

- before mission start; · while the mission is in progress; · after completion.

Opening or downloading a resource must not alter mission progress.

## Governing distinction

Mission Kit = what WLA gives the child.

Mission Kit does not contain the child's Mission Trail evidence.

A separate global download/library repository is not required for mission resources at this stage.

## 8. For Parents

For Parents sits separately from Mission Kit.

Its primary content is:

Mission Note for Parents

## Behaviour

The Parent Note remains accessible:

· before mission start; · during the mission; · after completion.

Opening it must not change mission state.

Parent guidance does not sit inside the normal child mission sequence unless a specific mission

genuinely requires adult involvement.

## Governing distinction

Mission Kit = materials used to do the mission.

For Parents = guidance for the adult enabling the mission.

## 9. Active Mission

The Active Mission is the doing environment.

It should be visually quieter and more task-focused than the public website.

The mission-specific Build Brief determines:

· screen sequence; · reveals; · branching; · responses; · physical/digital handoffs; · Mission Control states;

· completion logic.

## Shared Active Mission access

Where appropriate, the child should be able to reach:

· Mission Home; · Mission Kit; · Mission Control.

Leaving temporarily for one of these should not destroy mission state.

Leaving the Active Mission for Mission Home or Mission Kit must preserve the child’s exact

mission position and provide an obvious route back through Continue Mission.

## 10. Prepare

Where a mission requires physical resources, the Active Mission begins with a brief preparation state.

Preparation tells the child:

- what is needed now;

- whether anything needs printing;

- where to find those resources.

If resources are required:

Open Mission Kit

Once ready:

I'm ready

Preparation should orient, not repeat the Child Mission.

## 11. Screen ↔ physical handoffs

Hybrid missions may move deliberately between Academy and physical work.

At every Academy-to-physical handoff, the child should know:

Where do I go?

What do I do?

When do I come back?

The Academy should not duplicate physical work digitally unless the mission specifically requires a digital response or artefact.

## Governing principle

Use technology for orchestration. Preserve physical interaction for thinking, doing and evidence.

## 12. Mission Control

Mission Control is a reusable support component within the Active Mission.

It is available only where specified by the individual mission.

## Shared behaviour

Mission Control:

· opens without advancing the mission; · preserves the child's current state; · closes back to the same point; · displays only approved support; · does not reveal concealed information; · does not solve the mission for the child.

Mission-specific wording belongs in the Child Mission / individual Build Brief.

## 13. Pause and resume

Pause-and-return is standard Academy behaviour.

A child may legitimately leave the screen:

· to complete physical work; · for a short break; · until later that day; · until another day.

This should be treated as normal use.

## Required behaviour

When the child returns:

- mission status remains accurate; · completed digital actions persist; · established branches remain established; · revealed information remains revealed; · saved responses remain available; · Continue returns to the appropriate mission state.

A mission must not depend on one browser tab remaining open.

Individual Build Briefs specify which mission-specific state fields require persistence.

## 14. Mission Complete

When the mission's approved completion conditions are satisfied:

- mission status becomes Complete;

- a completion state is shown;

- Mission Home remains accessible;

- Mission Kit remains accessible;

- relevant Mission Trail information is surfaced;

- the child has a clear return to My Missions.

## Primary return

Back to My Missions

Completion should not be blocked by an upsell or purchase prompt.

## 15. Mission Trail

Mission Trail is the child's private record of selected evidence arising from missions.

It is not the same as Mission Kit.

## Mission Trail may include

## Physical evidence

Work the child keeps physically.

The Academy may identify that evidence as belonging to the Mission Trail without claiming to possess a digital copy.

## Digital evidence

Responses or artefacts genuinely stored by the Academy.

## Governing distinction

Mission Kit = what WLA provides.

Mission Trail = evidence generated through the child's practice.

Mission Trail is private by default.

Sharing beyond it is optional and subject to the appropriate parent/guardian permission model.

## 16. Mission Board

Mission Board is an Academy-wide shared-practice surface.

## Placement

Mission Board sits:

beneath Mission Home

as an optional secondary Academy destination.

It does not interrupt:

My Missions → Mission Home → Active Mission

## Purpose

Mission Board provides a window into selected, anonymised WLA practice beyond the child's

own mission.

It should not introduce:

- ranking; · leaderboards;

- comparison scores.

## Contribution principle

Sharing to Mission Board is not automatic.

Any contribution model must preserve:

- optional participation; · anonymisation; · parent/guardian permission where required; · WLA safeguarding/privacy standards.

Detailed submission and moderation behaviour can be specified when Mission Board is built.

## 17. Completed Mission Home

Reopening a completed mission returns to its Mission Home.

It should show:

- Complete status;

- Mission Kit;

- For Parents;

- available stored digital Mission Trail evidence where appropriate.

It should not automatically restart the mission.

Restart Mission remains a future product decision and should not be assumed in the core architecture.

## 18. Shared resource model

Each mission may have:

- zero or more Mission Kit resources;

- one Mission Note for Parents;

- mission-specific Academy content;

- zero or more stored digital Mission Trail items.

Resources should remain attached to the mission they belong to rather than collected in an undifferentiated global repository.

## 19. Shared mission-status model

At minimum, the Academy must know:

- which child profile the mission belongs to;

- whether that child has access;

- mission status;

- where the child last reached;

- whether the mission has been completed.

Mission-specific state is defined by the individual Build Brief.

The Academy Architecture should not force every mission into the same internal sequence.

## 20. Responsive accessibility

The Academy should support the intended child experience on:

- smartphone portrait;

- tablet portrait;

- tablet landscape.

Core behaviour should not depend on desktop use.

Shared interface expectations include:

· clear primary actions; · comfortable touch targets; · no hover-dependent core interaction; · no unnecessary precision interaction; · readable content; · no required horizontal scrolling; · status or state not communicated by colour alone.

Mission-specific responsive requirements remain downstream.

## 21. Architecture ownership boundaries

## Academy Architecture owns

Shared platform behaviour such as:

· My Missions; · Mission Home; · Mission Kit; · For Parents; · Active Mission shell; · pause and resume; · mission status; · Mission Trail distinction; · Mission Board placement; · common navigation behaviour.

## Individual Academy Build Brief owns

Mission-specific:

· screens; · reveals; · branches; · Mission Control content; · responses; · handoffs; · mission state; · completion behaviour;

· technical QA.

## Child Mission owns

Child-facing mission instructions.

## Mission Note for Parents owns

Mission-specific parent guidance.

## Governing rule

One idea. One authoritative home.

Future briefs should inherit shared Academy behaviour rather than repeat it.

## 22. Deferred decisions

The following do not need to be resolved within the core Academy architecture.

## Mission Board submission/moderation detail

Resolve when Mission Board enters build.

## Mission replay

Resolve after evidence from use.

## My Missions search/filter

Introduce only when mission volume creates the need.

## Notifications

Introduce only where a genuine user need emerges.

These should not expand the Academy architecture prematurely.
