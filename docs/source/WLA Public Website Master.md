## **Inside a mission**

A Mission is a problem or situation in front of your child.

**Something to do.**  
It asks for action, not just an answer.

**Something open.**  
Clear enough to begin. Open enough for more than one way through.

**Something that changes.**  
What they do shapes what happens next.

**Something to show for it.**  
A build. A test result. A changed plan. A decision trail. An explanation.

Over time, selected evidence builds the child’s private Mission Trail — a record of what they have tried, changed, noticed and worked out.

**The aim is not perfect work. It is practice you can see.**

# **6\. ABOUT**

## **Why WLA exists**

WLA is built on a simple idea: children become more capable when they have meaningful things to work through for themselves.

Explanation can help, but it can't replace the experience of making a decision, testing an idea, responding to change, or working out what to do next.

Missions give children enough direction to begin, without taking the thinking away.

## **Where WLA started**

Within Lab Academy grew from Creatively Navigating Hijrah — a blog rooted in writing, reflection and making sense of change.

Over time, a pattern kept returning: insight matters, but it's what we practise that shapes how we respond and what we're able to do.

That idea moved from reflection into experiences where children could think, act, adjust and learn from what happened.

**That became Within Lab Academy.**

## **Why “Within”?**

The name reflects where the practice ultimately lands.

A child may be building, deciding, questioning, planning or resetting on the outside. But through those experiences, they are also developing something within: judgement, confidence, self-management, responsibility, and the ability to respond for themselves.

# **8\. FULL FAQ**

## **Questions parents often ask**

### **What is a WLA mission?**

A WLA mission gives your child a real problem or situation to work through, at their own pace.

Depending on the mission, they might build, choose, investigate, test, plan, organise or work something out.

### **What ages is WLA for?**

WLA missions are designed for children aged 7–15.

Children can approach the same mission at different levels without needing a different activity for every age.

### **Do the Labs need to be completed in order?**

No.

There is no set sequence. Choose by interest, readiness or the kind of practice that feels useful right now.

### **How much parent help is needed?**

WLA is parent-supported, not parent-dependent.

You may help with access, preparation, ordinary materials or safety where needed. The meaningful work stays with the child.

### **Is WLA screen-based?**

WLA is screen-light.

Some missions use very little digital support. Others combine digital guidance with physical or real-world action.

### **Will we need to print anything?**

Yes. WLA missions are screen-light, so you’ll print the mission and any printable materials it includes.

**What materials will we need?**

It depends on the mission. WLA keeps physical materials ordinary and manageable. We show key requirements before purchase.

### **How long does a mission take?**

You’ll see an approximate duration on each mission page.

WLA is self-paced, so the time is a guide rather than a deadline.

### **Can we stop and come back later?**

Yes.

Children can pause a mission and come back later.

### **What is the Mission Board?**

No.

### **Does my child need to show their face?**

No.

WLA keeps children's participation private by default. They do not need to show their face, and mission evidence focuses on the work itself.

# **Safeguarding & Child Privacy**

Within Lab Academy builds safeguarding and privacy into mission design, digital delivery, evidence collection and any sharing of children’s work.

## **Child privacy**

WLA does not require child faces, public full names, school names, identifiable home information or unnecessary sensitive personal information to take part.

## **Mission Trail**

The Mission Trail is the child’s private record of evidence from their missions.

It is not a public profile.

## **Sharing work**

Sharing is optional and only happens if a parent or guardian chooses to share the child’s work.

WLA may also ask whether the child’s work can be shared.

When work is shared, WLA uses anonymised, object-led evidence.

## **Mission Board**

The Mission Board shows anonymised examples of work shared from across WLA.

It does not rank children or show identifying information.

## **Safety within missions**

WLA reviews each mission for physical, emotional and digital safety.

Where needed, the mission gives clear safety instructions and tells families when adult help is needed for safety.

If a mission raises a safety concern, the child should stop and involve a parent or guardian.

## **Parent or guardian help**

Some missions need limited adult help with preparation, access, materials or safety.

The mission makes clear when help is needed.

## **How WLA uses mission evidence**

WLA may look at mission evidence to improve missions, check quality and understand how they work in practice.

This is separate from sharing.

## **Concerns**

Parents and guardians can contact WLA about any safeguarding or privacy concern.

Safeguarding and privacy take priority over completing a mission.

## **Privacy Policy**

For information about how personal data is collected, used, stored and protected, see the Privacy Policy.

# **Accessibility**

Within Lab Academy designs its website, Academy and missions to be clear and usable for children and families.

Accessibility means reducing unnecessary barriers without lowering the thinking required by the mission.

## **Clear to use**

WLA provides:

- Clear, direct instructions
- Readable layouts
- Obvious next steps
- Simple digital interactions
- Clear movement between screen and physical work

**The challenge should come from the mission, not from working out how to use it.**

## **Different ways to take part**

Where appropriate, children may be able to:

- Read or listen
- Speak or write
- Point, select or draw
- Use physical materials
- Give a short digital response

**The access route may vary. The intended thinking does not.**

## **Devices, audio and print**

WLA designs its digital experience for common family devices, including smartphones and tablets.

Core actions should not depend on hover, precise movement, colour alone or unnecessary horizontal scrolling.

Where audio contains essential information, WLA also provides a text equivalent.

WLA missions require printing because they are screen-light and physical handling is part of the experience.

## **Need another route?**

If a mission, resource or Academy feature is difficult to access, contact WLA.

Where practical, WLA will consider an alternative route or adjustment that preserves the intended learning.

**Contact WLA →**

## **Ongoing improvement**

WLA reviews accessibility as the Academy and its missions develop.
